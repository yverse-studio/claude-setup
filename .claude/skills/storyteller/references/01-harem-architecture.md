# 01 · Harem rom-com architecture (the reusable skeleton)

Studied 2026-10-17: Girlfriend, Girlfriend · I Made Friends with the Second Prettiest Girl in My Class (Clamoku) · I'm Getting Married to a Girl I Hate in My Class · The Quintessential Quintuplets · Rent-A-Girlfriend · Nisekoi · Oregairu · Saekano · The Angel Next Door Spoils Me Rotten · My Dress-Up Darling · The Café Terrace and Its Goddesses · 100 Girlfriends · Horimiya · Alya Sometimes Hides Her Feelings in Russian · Date A Live · Infinite Stratos. Publisher note: Clamoku, Married-to-a-Girl-I-Hate and Alya are Kadokawa (Sneaker Bunko); Girlfriend Girlfriend, Quintuplets, Rent-A-Girlfriend and Café Terrace are Kodansha; the "Kadokawa vibe" yskills means is the Sneaker-Bunko school: one clear device, one lead heroine, small daily intimacy, light comedy, no cruelty. Sources: `sources.md`.

## The five parts every one of them has

1. **The device.** One premise that forces the lead and the heroines into the same room every chapter and makes every meeting plausible. Fake relationship (Nisekoi, Rent-A-Girlfriend), arranged marriage (Married), tutoring job (Quintuplets), cohabitation (Café Terrace, Angel Next Door), shared secret (Horimiya, Alya), a club (Oregairu), a project (Saekano, Dress-Up Darling), a mission (Date A Live), an explicit "date both" pact (Girlfriend Girlfriend). The device must (a) be funny on its own, (b) give a reason to meet daily, (c) carry a ticking question ("when does the fake become real / when is the secret found out").
2. **The lead heroine.** One heroine owns the spine. Her arc resolves the device's question. Everyone else orbits.
3. **The lineup.** Three to six heroines with non-overlapping archetypes (`02-cast-archetypes.md`), each with her own entry event, her own "why him", her own scene type she owns, and her own secret.
4. **The escalation ladder.** Small, countable intimacy steps, each a chapter beat, in roughly this order (`03-pacing-and-beats.md`).
5. **The endgame contract.** Decided before chapter 1: chosen-one (Quintuplets, Nisekoi, Horimiya), open-but-lead (Oregairu, Saekano), or all-stay (Girlfriend Girlfriend, 100 Girlfriends). The contract decides whether rival routes close or stay warm. **The company story is all-stay** (yskills 2026-10-17): a real romance with every woman, they come to get along over time, and all end up happy together with him. All-stay needs what 100 Girlfriends does right: each woman's romance is written in full on its own before the group exists, and the women's relationships with each other get their own rungs (rival → truce → ally → the pact; compressed by the harem rule below: it plays as friendly rivalry from the start).

## Act structure (serialized, 10 chapters per heroine arc)

| Act | Chapters | Job | Harem-specific move |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Setup | 1 to 2 | Device fires, lead heroine meets him, first "no way" | Introduce at most two heroines. Each new face gets her own small scene, never a group intro. |
| 2 Build | 3 to 6 | Repeating daily scenes, one intimacy rung per chapter, one new heroine per two chapters | The comedic engine runs here: misunderstanding, double booking, jealousy, secret keeping. |
| 3 Pressure | 7 to 8 | Rival moves, a secret leaks, the device threatens to end | The lead heroine's doubt beat. One rival gets her spotlight chapter. |
| 4 Commit | 9 to 10 | Confession or its equivalent, the night, the new normal | Chosen-one: rivals get a graceful close. All-stay: the pact scene instead of a choice. |

Serialized harems add heroines through events, never in a batch (Date A Live: one spirit per arc; 100 Girlfriends: a few per season; Café Terrace: five in one house but each gets an opening chapter). Rule of thumb from the titles: a new heroine every 2 to 3 chapters in act 2, none in act 4.

## The common route, then branches (visual-novel model)

Clannad, Fate and every eroge use the same shape and it fits a game better than a linear novel:

- **Common route** (chapters 1 to 4 of the company story): every heroine is introduced, affection accrues from small choices, nobody is locked.
- **Branch point**: the first choice that costs something (going to one woman's event means missing another's). From here each heroine has her own route of 4 to 6 chapters.
- **Route lock**: a commit event (first kiss with intent, "stay the night") sets `route=<slug>`; rival romance storylets close, rival friendship storylets stay open so the world keeps breathing.
- **True route / harem route** (optional): unlocked only after N single routes are done, or by a pact scene the lineup agrees to (Girlfriend Girlfriend's logic: the lead heroine consents on page, with a reason true to her).

## Comedic engines (pick one per chapter, two at most)

- **Misunderstanding** with a real cause (she saw him with her sister). Must resolve inside two chapters or readers call it stalling (the "dense protagonist" complaint is the genre's most hated flaw).
- **Double booking** (two invitations, same evening).
- **Playful jealousy** that she hides badly (body language carries it, dialogue denies it) and that resolves in the same scene; in a harem story it never blocks a route (harem rule below).
- **Secret keeping** with a near-miss per chapter (Horimiya, Alya).
- **Role reversal** (the composed one loses composure; the slob is competent at one thing).
- **The pact** (Girlfriend Girlfriend): rules the characters set for themselves and then bend.

## The harem rule (yskills 2026-10-27, final; every harem story, one home is this section)

- **The harem dynamic starts early.** The first teasing beat by chapter 2 or 3 and the second woman on page by chapter 2 to 4. Women notice each other from the start and the lead's circle is visibly shared well before the midpoint; the shared-circle feel is a reason to read, not a payoff held back for the finale.
- **All the women are fine with sharing.** No woman demands he choose, no woman leaves, sulks for a chapter or blocks another's route. Jealousy is allowed only as a playful tell: teasing, a competitive streak, a pout that resolves in the same scene, a bet. It never blocks a rung, never costs a friendship, and is never hurt left standing at a chapter end. Rivalry stays play (who gets him for lunch, who wins the bet), warm underneath.
- **Sharing is said out loud, early, and means it.** The admission and the rule rungs of the second ladder (`03`) are comic and affectionate and may come in the first third; each woman's reason to be fine with it is true to her sheet, never a plot device.
- **Her romance is still her own.** Each woman keeps her own full ladder and her own night; sharing adds scenes between the women, it removes none of hers. All characters adult, on page and on the sheet, as everywhere.

## What's dated vs still loved (2026)

Dated: the dense lead who cannot read a confession, accidental groping, bath-walk-in as the only fan service, heroines with no life outside him, a misunderstanding held for ten chapters, the little-sister archetype played for romance (out of our content line anyway: all cast adults, no family romance in the company story).

Still loved: a decisive lead (Girlfriend Girlfriend, Alya's Masachika), heroines with jobs and goals (Dress-Up Darling's Marin, Saekano's Utaha), the daily-life cosiness (cooking, umbrella, texting), honest all-stay endings written with buildup for every party, a lead heroine whose "why him" is specific and shown.

## Scene types a harem story is made of (the catalogue)

Daily: shared commute · lunch · after-work drink · cooking at his or her place · the rainy day and the one umbrella · sick day care · late-night texting · the lift stuck or the last train missed. Set pieces: company outing (the Japanese cultural-festival slot) · beach or lake day · hot spring / spa · sleepover after a party · the business trip with one hotel room · the wedding of a colleague · Christmas market and New Year. Private: first time at hers · the morning after · the shower · the bed scene itself (`04-style-presets.md`, preset "filthy"/"tender").

Each heroine owns one scene type nobody else gets (the gym girl owns sick-day care, the cook owns cooking). Reuse a scene type only with a twist.
