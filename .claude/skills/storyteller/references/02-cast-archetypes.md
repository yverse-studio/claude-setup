# 02 · Cast archetypes and the cliché each one owns

A harem lineup works when every heroine answers four questions differently: **why him**, **what she hides**, **which scene type she owns**, **how she shows jealousy**. Archetype gives the first guess; the character sheet (`05-pipeline.md`, cast file) makes it a person. All cast adults, all original (project rule).

| Archetype | Core loop | Jealousy tell | Owns the scene | Classic rung order | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tsundere** (Nisekoi Chitoge, Married's Akane) | insults as affection, softens in private | louder insults, then silence | the argument that turns into a walk home | hostility → accidental kindness → caught being kind → denial → the one honest line | insults with no warmth underneath |
| **Kuudere / ice queen** (Oregairu Yukino, Saekano Utaha) | composed, precise, allows one crack per chapter | over-precise questions about the other woman | the late office, the one lamp | respect → tea → a joke only he gets → a request phrased as an order | a crack every scene (then she's not cool) |
| **Childhood friend / the one who was always there** (Girlfriend Girlfriend Hina, Nisekoi Kosaki) | shared history, first-name basis from page one, afraid to change the status quo | goes quiet, "I'm happy for you" | cooking at his place, the old photo | comfort → jealousy she denies → the memory → the confession she thought he knew | losing on purpose (she must fight, or the reader hates the lead) |
| **Kouhai / junior** (Rent-A-Girlfriend Ruka energy, many) | open admiration, forward, pushes the pace | sulks visibly, texts more | the break-room ambush, the "teach me" | admiration → forward move → he sets a boundary → she respects it and gets more effective | writing her as a child (she is an adult colleague who is junior in rank only) |
| **Senpai / older woman** (Café Terrace Riho vibe, boss types) | competent, teasing, in control until she isn't | pretends not to notice, then pulls rank | the business trip, the bar after hours | mentoring → a drink → the first time she asks instead of tells → her vulnerability | making her only a mentor |
| **Gyaru / the loud one** (Dress-Up Darling Marin) | bright, physical, direct, no shame, a secret soft side | theatrical pouting, then real hurt | the beach day, the fitting room | friendly touching → his competence impresses her → she gets shy for the first time | treating her directness as "easy" |
| **Class rep / organiser** (Alya, student-council types) | rules, lists, perfectionism, a private second language of feelings | corrects the rival in public | the planning meeting, the schedule that includes him | efficiency → he sees her off-duty → the second language (what she says when she thinks he can't hear) | no off-duty self |
| **The quiet one / secret life** (Horimiya Hori, Angel Next Door Mahiru) | plain at work, different at home | none shown, then an unexpected act | her apartment, the care scene | a glimpse of the other self → he keeps the secret → she lets him in | the reveal as the whole arc (it's the start) |
| **Rival / enemies to lovers** (Married's Akane and Saito) | competition is the language of attraction | sabotage that helps him | the contest, the forced pairing | rivalry → forced proximity → shared win → the first "we" | a rival who is only mean |
| **The friend's friend / the honest one** (Clamoku Umi, Married's Himari) | asks for friendship first, means it, catches feelings | guilt, loyalty conflict | the video shop, the plain hangout | friendship pact → the pact strains → honesty about it | pretending the friendship was fake |
| **The fake partner** (Rent-A-Girlfriend Chizuru, Nisekoi) | professional distance, a contract, real feelings leak | denies having a stake | every public date | performance → one unscripted moment → the contract ends → what's left | dragging the fake past its natural end |
| **The wild card / god of love** (100 Girlfriends, Date A Live) | the premise itself: each heroine is an arc, each arc a rescue | n/a | the arc's own set piece | meet → crisis → the seal (kiss/confession) → joins the household | arcs that repeat the same crisis |

## The men around him

- **The best friend / wingman** (Saekano's crew, Married's Himari as a female version): knows the score, pushes, occasionally wants one of them himself. One per story, never two.
- **The rival male**: optional, short-lived, exists to make a heroine admit what she feels. Never wins.
- **The protagonist**: decisive and honest is the 2026 default (Girlfriend Girlfriend, Alya); dense is dated. He can be clumsy, broke, overworked, but never blind. He says what he wants; the plot comes from what it costs.

## Lineup rules

- No two heroines share a jealousy tell or an owned scene type.
- Each heroine needs one hard "no" she can say to him and a reason she'd still come back.
- Each has a life that continues off-page (job, family, a hobby that produces plot).
- Introduce the lead heroine first and alone. Introduce a rival only when the lead heroine's rung is one step ahead.
